New Jersey Solar Journey

A D3.js narrative visualization — 25 years of solar policy told as a story you can walk through, then explore on your own.

Data Visualization Project

How Far Has New Jersey Come on Its Solar Journey?

A line chart alone will not tell you why a curve bends. This project turns two decades of New Jersey solar installation data into a guided five-scene story — each scene framed by the policy that shaped it, from the 2001 rebate program through the SREC boom to Executive Order 315's pledge of 100% clean electricity by 2035. The reader advances through the argument scene by scene, and at every stop the chart stays fully interactive: click any year for its exact capacity, jump to any era, or open the expert commentary on what the numbers are hiding.

Project Overview

The story the data tells

The rise: from 23 kW of installed capacity in 2001 to 4.4 GW by 2023 — New Jersey spent most of two decades ahead of its own published targets, holding #4 nationally through the SREC years.

The turn: in 2023 the state fell out of the national Top 10 for the first time in twenty years — just as its new target demands 17 GW by 2035, roughly 1.05 GW every year, about triple the pace of the decade behind it.

The visualization follows the interactive slideshow pattern: a fixed authored sequence of five scenes, each holding the reader inside one policy era, with free exploration available within every scene rather than only at the end. Scene state drives everything — the year domain, the annotation, the highlighted endpoints, the computed growth rate — so the story and the chart can never drift out of step.

The Challenge

Twenty-five years of a single monotonically rising curve looks like uninterrupted success. The interesting question — is New Jersey ahead of or behind where it promised to be? — is invisible unless the promise is drawn alongside the outcome, and unless the reader is told which policy was in force when each bend happened.

My Approach

I plotted the state's published capacity goals as a dashed reference line under the actual installations, then shaded the area between them wherever reality ran ahead of the plan — making overachievement, and its later disappearance, readable at a glance. Around that, five scenes each carry an annotation, a national ranking, and an expert-insight panel, so every visible inflection is attached to the decision that caused it.

Key Features

Everything between the raw capacity numbers and a reader who leaves understanding what happened — the sequence, the reference line, the annotations, and the freedom to go check any of it directly.

Five-Scene Guided Sequence

Each policy era — the Clean Energy Program, the SREC launch, the Solar Act, the Successor Incentive, and the 2035 Energy Master Plan — is its own scene, reachable with Next/Previous or by jumping straight to a period from the timeline buttons.

Promise Plotted Against Outcome

Actual cumulative installations run as a solid line over the state's published goals as a dashed one, with the region between them filled wherever installations exceeded plan — so a shrinking green band signals a state losing its lead before any caption says so.

Annotations and Expert Commentary

Consistent d3-annotation labels mark the milestone behind each era, a national-ranking line sits above the chart, and a toggleable expert-insight panel supplies the interpretation — the trade-off, the risk, the comparison to peer states — that the curve alone cannot carry.

Per-Scene Growth Rate

Every scene computes and displays the growth rate across its own span, with the opening and closing years marked directly on the line — giving each era a single comparable number instead of leaving the reader to eyeball slopes.

Exploration Inside the Story

The line draws itself in on entry, then hands control over: any data point can be clicked to pin a tooltip with its exact capacity, and multiple pins can stay open at once for comparison — drill-down available in every scene, not only after the narrative ends.

Cleaned, Reconciled Data

Annual and cumulative capacity series were derived from national installation records and reconciled against New Jersey's published goals, with interpolation filling the gap between the last stated target and the 17 GW commitment for 2035.
