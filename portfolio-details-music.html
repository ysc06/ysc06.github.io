Music Recommender Simulation

A machine learning recommender small enough to read end to end — built AI-assisted for CodePath’s AI Engineering course, with every score designed, every ranking explained, and every bias written down.

Machine Learning — Recommender Systems

TuneMatch 1.0 — Turning Taste Into a Ranking, and Saying Why

Spotify's ranking logic is invisible by design; this one is not. I built a content-based recommender in pure Python that walks the standard machine learning recommender pipeline — represent each song as a feature vector, encode a listener as a four-field taste profile, score the catalog, rank it, then evaluate what the ranking got wrong. The scoring function is designed rather than fitted, which is the point: an interpretable baseline you can defend line by line before any weights get learned. Every recommendation ships with a sentence explaining which signals earned it its place, and the accompanying model card names the failure modes that same weighting creates.

Project Overview

The scoring rule, in numbers

Genre is the anchor at 35 points, mood adds up to 30, energy proximity up to 25, and acoustic texture nudges the total by ±10 — a deliberate choice that taste labels should outrank a perfect numerical fit.

For “The Focused Studier” profile, Focus Flow clears every signal at once and scores a perfect 100.00; the next three are lofi tracks whose moods are adjacent to focused, landing in the 86–88 band on partial mood credit.

Each of the 18 catalog songs carries ten fields — genre, mood, energy, tempo, valence, danceability, acousticness and identifiers — loaded from CSV with numeric coercion at the boundary. A UserProfile compresses a listener into four: favorite genre, favorite mood, target energy, and whether they like acoustic. Scoring compares the two, sorts descending, and returns the top k, defaulting to five.

Built for CodePath's AI Engineering course (AI 110) as an AI-assisted project: I used an AI coding assistant throughout — drafting the scoring function, pressure-testing the weights against edge cases, and interrogating my own design for the biases that ended up in the model card — while keeping the modeling decisions, and the responsibility for them, mine.

The Challenge

A recommender with no learned component has nowhere to hide: the weights are the model. Choosing them means committing, in advance, to a claim about what actually predicts whether someone likes a song — and then owning the recommendations that claim produces, including the bad ones.

My Approach

I made genre the anchor and demoted the continuous features to tie-breakers, on the reasoning that a slightly-too-energetic lofi track beats a perfectly-paced track from a genre the listener avoids. Then I softened the sharpest edge with mood adjacency — chill earns partial credit against focused — and audited the rest in a model card instead of pretending they were solved.

Key Features

Everything between a CSV of songs and a ranked list a listener can argue with — the feature model, the weighting, the explanations, and an honest account of where it breaks.

Weighted Multi-Signal Scoring

Genre contributes 35 points, mood up to 30, energy proximity up to 25, and acoustic preference a ±10 adjustment — a fixed budget that makes the model's priorities inspectable rather than emergent.

Mood Adjacency, Not Exact Match

Rather than treating mood as a binary hit, related moods earn partial credit — chill and relaxed both score against a focused profile — so near-misses surface instead of collapsing to zero alongside genuinely wrong songs.

An Explanation With Every Result

Each recommendation is returned with a generated sentence naming the signals that earned it — matched genre, adjacent mood, energy 0.42 against a target of 0.40, acoustic texture — so a surprising ranking can be traced instead of guessed at.

Profile-Driven Simulation

Swapping the four-field UserProfile reruns the whole catalog under a different listener, making it cheap to test how the same weights behave for a studier, a runner, or someone whose taste the catalog barely covers.

Documented Failure Modes

The model card records what the weighting costs: genre dominance means a great mood-and-energy match in the “wrong” genre can never win; single-song genres leave those listeners with filler; indie pop and pop are strangers to a string comparison; and tempo, valence and danceability sit unused in the data.

Tested and Reproducible

A pytest suite pins the loader and the scoring behavior, and the run is a single python -m src.main against a checked-in catalog — the documented sample output is reproducible rather than illustrative.

AI-Assisted Engineering Workflow

Developed with an AI coding assistant as a working partner — drafting and refactoring the scoring function, generating adversarial profiles to probe the weights, and challenging the design until the failure modes were specific enough to write down. The modeling calls stayed mine; the assistant made it faster to find out where they were wrong.
